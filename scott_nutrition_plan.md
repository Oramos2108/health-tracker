**Protein timing note:** Adderall typically suppresses morning appetite. Do not skip breakfast. A protein-rich breakfast — even if it is a quick Greek yogurt bowl or a 2-egg scramble — blunts the blood sugar rise from any carbs you eat later and stabilizes energy through a morning shift. Aim for at least 25–35 g of protein at breakfast, lunch, and dinner, with snacks filling in the rest.

## Biomarker-Tailored Strategy

**Metabolic health / A1C 5.7%**
Your A1C of 5.7% sits exactly at the [ADA's prediabetes threshold](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis), and the good news is that dietary changes alone can move it. The strategy here is straightforward: replace refined grains with whole grain or legume-based carbs, pair every carb source with protein and fiber to blunt the glucose curve, and eat your vegetables and protein before the carb portion of any meal (sometimes called the "food order" trick — strong evidence supports a 20–30% reduction in post-meal glucose when vegetables and protein are eaten first). A tablespoon of apple cider vinegar in water before a starchy meal produces a similar effect and is worth building into your routine. No white bread, white rice, or fruit juice on their own.

**Gut health**
The gut microbiome responds most dramatically to dietary variety. Research published in [Nutrients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7822000/) confirms that Mediterranean-style eating — high fiber, diverse plants, fermented foods — shifts the gut toward beneficial strains and increases short-chain fatty acid production. Aim for 30+ distinct plant foods per week (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, herbs, and spices all count, as noted by [Emory Healthy Aging](https://healthyaging.emory.edu/could-eating-30-plants-a-week-be-the-answer-to-better-health/)). Include at least one fermented food daily: plain Greek yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, or kimchi. Prebiotic fibers from oats, onions, garlic, leeks, and asparagus feed beneficial bacteria.

**Vitamin D deficiency**
At 20 ng/mL, you are below the 30 ng/mL sufficiency threshold identified by [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/). The best food sources are fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel — 2–3 times per week), egg yolks, mushrooms that have been sun-exposed or UV-treated, and fortified dairy products. Food alone is unlikely to fully correct a deficiency at 20 ng/mL — discuss a supplemental dose of 1,000–2,000 IU daily vitamin D3 with your PCP and recheck levels in 3 months.

**Iron and hemoglobin**
Lean red meat (grass-fed beef or lamb, 1–2 times per week) provides heme iron with the highest bioavailability. Sardines, chicken thighs, and lentils round out the iron sources. Pair plant-based iron sources with vitamin C (bell pepper, citrus, strawberries) at the same meal to enhance non-heme iron absorption — [PubMed research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6940487/) confirms ascorbic acid forms a soluble chelate with iron that stays absorbable at intestinal pH. Avoid coffee or tea within 1 hour of iron-rich meals, as polyphenols in both beverages can reduce non-heme iron absorption by 50–90% depending on quantity consumed.

**Kidney stone prevention**
The single most important intervention is drinking 2.5–3 L of water daily to keep urine dilute. The [National Kidney Foundation](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/kidney-stone-diet-plan-and-prevention) recommends eating calcium-rich foods with oxalate-rich foods rather than avoiding calcium — dietary calcium binds oxalate in the gut and prevents it from reaching the kidneys. Practical examples: yogurt with berries, cheese on a salad that contains greens. Limit very high-oxalate foods in large portions (spinach, almonds, beets, rhubarb) but do not cut them entirely. Add citrate daily via lemon juice squeezed into water or over food — citrate inhibits stone formation. Sodium under 2,300 mg reduces urinary calcium excretion.

**LDL maintenance**
LDL dropped from 160 to 102 mg/dL in two years — an outstanding result. Protect it by continuing the foods responsible: oily fish for omega-3s, EVOO and avocado for monounsaturated fat, and soluble fiber from oats, legumes, and apples. The [AHA](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cholesterol/prevention-and-treatment-of-high-cholesterol-hyperlipidemia) confirms that omega-3 fatty acids and replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat are the strongest dietary drivers of LDL reduction. Keep almonds moderate due to their oxalate content; favor walnuts and pistachios instead.

**Sebopsoriasis**
Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA from salmon, sardines, and walnuts) have the strongest evidence for reducing inflammatory skin conditions — a comprehensive review cited in [Frontiers in Immunology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7892455/) found improvement in 12 of 15 psoriasis trials. Polyphenol-rich foods (berries, olive oil, colorful vegetables), turmeric in cooking, and avoiding ultra-processed foods and alcohol all reduce the inflammatory substrate that drives flares. You are in alcohol remission — this matters enormously for skin inflammation.

## 7-Day Meal Plan

### Monday

**Breakfast — Greek Yogurt Power Bowl (~480 kcal | P 32g · C 52g · F 14g · Fiber 8g)**
- 1 cup plain whole-milk Greek yogurt (Vit D-fortified if available)
- 1/2 cup mixed berries (fresh or frozen, thawed)
- 2 Tbsp ground flaxseed
- 1/4 cup walnuts, roughly chopped
- 1 Tbsp honey, drizzle

Prep: Layer yogurt in a bowl. Top with berries, flaxseed, walnuts, and honey. Done in 2 minutes.

**Lunch — Sardine and White Bean Bowl (~520 kcal | P 38g · C 44g · F 16g · Fiber 12g)**
- 1 can (3.75 oz) sardines in olive oil, drained (reserve 1 tsp oil)
- 1 cup canned white beans, rinsed
- 1/2 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
- 1/2 cup cucumber, diced
- Juice of 1/2 lemon
- Salt and pepper to taste

Prep: Combine beans, tomatoes, and cucumber in a bowl. Flake sardines over the top. Drizzle with lemon juice and the reserved sardine oil. Toss gently. (Sardines hit Vit D, iron, and omega-3 in one can.)

**Dinner — Sheet Pan Salmon with Sweet Potato and Broccoli (~640 kcal | P 46g · C 52g · F 20g · Fiber 9g)**
- 6 oz wild salmon fillet
- 1 medium sweet potato, cubed (about 1 cup)
- 1.5 cups broccoli florets
- 1.5 Tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp garlic powder, salt, pepper

Prep: Preheat oven to 425°F. Toss sweet potato cubes in 1 Tbsp olive oil, salt, and garlic powder on a sheet pan. Roast 15 minutes. Push to the side, add broccoli and salmon. Drizzle remaining oil over salmon. Roast 12–14 more minutes until salmon is cooked through.

**Snack 1 — Apple with Peanut Butter (~280 kcal | P 8g · C 34g · F 14g · Fiber 5g)**
- 1 medium apple, sliced
- 2 Tbsp natural peanut butter

**Snack 2 — Kefir (~140 kcal | P 10g · C 12g · F 3g · Fiber 0g)**
- 1 cup plain low-fat kefir (gut health, Vit D-fortified)

**Monday Total: ~2,060 kcal | P ~134g · C ~194g · F ~67g · Fiber ~34g**
(Add a slice of whole grain bread at lunch or an extra handful of walnuts at dinner to reach 2,400 kcal if needed.)

### Tuesday

**Breakfast — Scrambled Eggs with Avocado Toast (~500 kcal | P 28g · C 38g · F 24g · Fiber 9g)**
- 3 large eggs, scrambled
- 2 slices whole grain bread, toasted
- 1/2 medium avocado, sliced
- Salt, pepper, red pepper flakes

Prep: Scramble eggs in a non-stick pan over medium-low heat with a little olive oil spray, 3–4 minutes. Toast bread. Top each slice with avocado and eggs.

**Lunch — Chicken and Lentil Soup (~560 kcal | P 44g · C 52g · F 12g · Fiber 14g)**
- 5 oz cooked chicken breast (rotisserie is fine), shredded
- 1 cup canned green lentils, rinsed
- 1.5 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 1/2 cup diced carrots
- 1/2 cup diced celery
- 1 tsp cumin, salt, pepper

Prep: Combine broth, carrots, celery in a small pot. Simmer 10 minutes. Add lentils and chicken. Heat through 5 minutes. Season with cumin, salt, pepper. (Lentils are one of the best plant-based iron sources — pair with a small glass of orange juice to enhance non-heme iron absorption.)

**Dinner — Grass-Fed Beef Stir-Fry with Brown Rice (~660 kcal | P 46g · C 62g · F 18g · Fiber 6g)**
- 5 oz lean grass-fed ground beef or sirloin strips
- 1 cup cooked brown rice
- 1 cup bell peppers (mixed colors), sliced
- 1 cup snap peas or green beans
- 2 Tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp olive oil

Prep: Heat olive oil in a pan over high heat. Brown beef 5–6 minutes, breaking apart. Remove beef, add vegetables to the same pan. Stir-fry 4 minutes. Return beef. Add soy sauce and sesame oil. Serve over rice. (Bell peppers are high in Vit C — this pairing maximizes iron absorption from the beef and the rice.)

**Snack — Cottage Cheese with Pineapple (~220 kcal | P 22g · C 20g · F 4g · Fiber 1g)**
- 3/4 cup low-fat cottage cheese
- 1/2 cup fresh or canned pineapple chunks (in juice, not syrup)

**Tuesday Total: ~1,940 kcal | P ~140g · C ~172g · F ~58g · Fiber ~30g**
(Add 1 oz walnuts and a medium banana as an extra snack to reach target calories.)

### Substitutions

**If you do not like sardines:** Use canned mackerel in olive oil (similar omega-3 and Vit D profile) or canned wild-caught salmon. Both are lower cost alternatives that hit the same nutritional targets.

**If you do not like Greek yogurt:** Use skyr (Icelandic-style strained yogurt — nearly identical macros, often slightly higher protein), plain kefir as a drinkable substitute, or firm silken tofu blended smooth as a dairy-free base.

**If you do not like salmon:** Use Arctic char (similar fat profile and Vit D), mackerel, or trout — all are fatty fish that provide EPA/DHA and Vitamin D. Canned options work just as well as fresh.

**If you do not like lentils:** Swap in canned black beans, pinto beans, or chickpeas cup for cup. Iron content is similar; adjust vitamin C pairing accordingly.

**If you do not like kefir:** Use plain kombucha (lower protein but probiotic) or miso soup (1 tsp white miso in hot water — gut-friendly, low calorie, easy).

**If whole grain bread bothers your digestion:** Use properly fermented sourdough (long-fermented varieties are lower in FODMAPs and have a lower glycemic index than regular whole wheat bread) or swap for sweet potato as your carb base.

**If you want a faster breakfast on Adderall mornings:** Blend 1 cup kefir + 1 scoop plain protein powder + 1/2 cup frozen berries + 1 Tbsp flaxseed into a shake. Drink within 30 minutes of taking Adderall. Hits 30+ g protein and takes 90 seconds.

## Weekly Habits and Rules of Thumb

1. **Drink 2.5–3 L of water daily.** This is the single most important habit for kidney stone prevention. Start each morning with a large glass of lemon water (2 Tbsp lemon juice) before coffee. Keep a 32 oz water bottle at work and finish it twice per shift.

2. **Eat one fermented food every day.** Greek yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, or miso all count. The [Mediterranean diet evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7822000/) is clear that daily fermented food consistently shifts the gut microbiome toward beneficial strains. This does not need to be a large portion — 1/2 cup of kimchi or 3/4 cup of yogurt is sufficient.

3. **Put protein on every plate.** Breakfast, lunch, dinner, and at least one snack. Protein blunts the post-meal glucose spike from carbohydrates, keeps you full through a long RN shift, and protects your lean body mass at 146 lbs.

4. **Use the plate method at dinner.** Fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, peppers, asparagus, Brussels sprouts, cabbage). Fill one quarter with a protein source. Fill the last quarter with a smart carb (brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato, lentils). This structure naturally hits the macro targets without counting.

5. **Pair calcium with oxalate foods every time.** Do not eat berries, greens, or sweet potato on their own. Add yogurt, a slice of cheese, or a splash of milk nearby. Per the [National Kidney Foundation](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/kidney-stone-diet-plan-and-prevention), dietary calcium binds gut oxalate and prevents it from reaching the kidneys — eating them together is protective, not harmful.

6. **Eat vegetables and protein before carbs at any meal.** This is one of the most evidence-supported non-drug interventions for glycemic control. Even a 5-minute difference between eating your salad and then your rice produces a measurable reduction in post-meal glucose.

7. **No alcohol.** You are in remission. Even one drink elevates systemic inflammation, disrupts sleep architecture, spikes cortisol, and is a documented trigger for sebopsoriasis flares. Every recipe in this plan is designed without it, and there are no replacements needed — lemon water, herbal tea, and sparkling water with lime cover every social situation.

## What to Watch: Recheck With Your Doctor

**Vitamin D supplement.** At 20 ng/mL, food alone may not fully correct your deficiency. Ask your PCP about starting 1,000–2,000 IU of vitamin D3 daily and rechecking your 25-OH Vitamin D in 3 months. Per [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/), maintenance doses in this range are safe and appropriate for adults not achieving sufficiency through diet and sun alone.

**A1C recheck in 3–6 months.** Your 5.7% A1C is dietary-responsive. Three to six months of the low-GI, high-fiber, protein-at-every-meal approach described here should produce a measurable reduction. The [ADA recommends](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis) repeat testing at least annually for those in the prediabetes range; given active dietary intervention, 3 months is reasonable.

**Lipid panel recheck in 6 months.** Your LDL of 102 mg/dL is a significant improvement from 160 mg/dL in 2024. Continue the dietary pattern that produced it (omega-3s, soluble fiber, reduced saturated fat) and verify with labs in 6 months that it is holding or improving further.

**Iron and ferritin recheck.** Your hemoglobin of 13.8 g/dL is mildly below the male reference range. Ask your PCP to check a full iron panel including ferritin, serum iron, and TIBC at your next visit. If ferritin is low, you may need a short course of supplemental iron — in which case the coffee/tea timing rules in this plan become especially important.

**Hydration and kidney stone monitoring.** If you experience any flank pain, hematuria, or urinary changes, contact your PCP. Continue targeting 2.5–3 L of water daily. If you pass another stone, request a 24-hour urine collection test (Litholink or equivalent) — this identifies the specific metabolic cause and allows truly personalized stone-prevention management per [NKF guidance](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/calcium-kidney-stones).

**Blood pressure trend.** Your 128/70 mmHg is in the elevated range by current AHA/ACC guidelines. The DASH-aligned pattern in this plan (high potassium from sweet potato, banana, and avocado; moderate sodium; minimal processed food) should help. Recheck at a routine visit in 6–12 months.
